01 / The big idea

One maths lesson. Three connected lenses.

These frameworks are not policy words to decorate a lesson. Each one has a distinct job in a meaningful learning experience.

NEP 2020
WHY

What kind of learner are we developing?

Curious, confident children who understand concepts, apply knowledge, communicate, collaborate and solve real problems.

NCF
HOW

How should learning happen?

Through developmentally appropriate, active and joyful teaching: objects, pictures, conversation, games and reflection.

HPC
EVIDENCE

How do we notice progress?

Observe what each child can do, explain and apply. Record strengths and next steps, not just a final score.

NEP sets the direction → NCF guides the learning design → HPC captures evidence of growth
02 / The learning shift

Before vs. after: what changes for the child?

These are illustrative teaching patterns. Not every classroom before 2020 was identical, and not every classroom today has fully implemented the policy vision.

A more traditional pattern

“Learn the rule. Repeat the sum.”

  • Teacher writes 24 + 13 on the board.
  • Children copy steps and practise similar sums.
  • Success is judged mainly by the correct answer.
  • If a child is wrong, they may be told to practise more without exploring the misconception.
A NEP-aligned learning experience

“Build it. Explain it. Use it.”

  • Children combine 24 counters and 13 counters.
  • They bundle ten ones into a ten, draw the model, then write 24 + 13 = 37.
  • They explain how they know and compare strategies.
  • Teacher observes reasoning, language, collaboration and next learning needs.

Important nuance: NEP 2020 is a policy vision, not a switch that instantly transformed every school. Implementation varies by state, school, resources and teacher preparation.

03 / Frameworks in action

Connect every classroom move to a purpose.

Use this mapping when you explain your demonstration to the Zoom audience.

🧭
NEP · Vision

Meaningful, experiential learning

Children use maths in familiar contexts: shopping, sharing food, arranging objects and planning a class event.

Ask:

Can the child use maths beyond the worksheet?

🧩
NCF · Pedagogy

Concrete → pictorial → abstract

Start with blocks or counters, sketch the model, then connect it to symbols such as +, −, × and ÷.

Ask:

What experience will make the idea understandable?

🔎
HPC · Assessment

Notice process, not only answer

Listen to explanations, observe strategy choice, note collaboration and invite reflection.

Ask:

What evidence shows the child understands?

Choose the developmental stage

Foundational: Count real objects, make small groups, compare “more / less”, and share toys equally. Keep it playful, oral, concrete and short. Do not rush formal algorithms.

OPERATION 01

The fruit stall: Addition

Combining two quantities to find a total

🍎
Real-life challenge

The fruit stall gets a delivery

Aarav has 4 apples. Maya brings 3 more apples to the stall. How many apples are there altogether?

4 + 3 = 7

Model: join both groups. Count all the objects.

Learning objective

Understand the meaning behind each operation

Teacher prompt
“What do you notice when we bring the two groups together? How can we prove the total?”
Look for evidence

Child counts accurately, combines groups, explains that addition joins quantities and represents the total.

    What HPC helps us notice

    • Conceptual understanding, not just speed.
    • Strategies, reasoning and representation.
    • Language, confidence and participation.
    • Progress over time and support needed next.

    What HPC is not

    • Not a label that defines a child.
    • Not only a marksheet with extra columns.
    • Not a one-time judgement.
    • Not a reason to compare children's worth.
    06 / Zoom run-of-show

    A 12-minute demonstration that feels alive.

    Let your audience experience the lesson, then reveal the policy connection behind each teaching move.

    Suggested timing
    0:00–1:00 · Hook

    “If I give you 5 blocks and then 3 more, how will you help a child discover the answer?”

    1:00–2:30 · Before / after

    Contrast copying an algorithm with building, drawing, explaining and applying.

    2:30–7:30 · Role-play

    Assign teacher, shopkeeper and learner. Act out an operation with counters.

    7:30–9:30 · Framework reveal

    Point out NEP vision, NCF teaching design and HPC evidence.

    9:30–11:00 · Assessment

    Use the rubric to name what you observed and give one next step.

    11:00–12:00 · Reflection

    Ask: “What did the child understand that a worksheet alone might not reveal?”

    Ready-to-say presenter script

    Opening statement

    “Today I am not going to teach four mathematical symbols only. I am going to show how children can experience addition, subtraction, multiplication and division through a simple market role-play. NEP 2020 gives us the broader vision of meaningful and experiential learning. NCF helps us design the learning journey, and HPC helps us notice the child's progress in understanding, reasoning, communication and collaboration.”

    Closing statement

    “The answer matters, but the child's thinking matters too. When a child builds the groups, explains the strategy and applies it in daily life, mathematics becomes meaningful. Our role is to create the experience, observe the learning and decide the next step.”

    Zoom tip: Hold real blocks, bottle caps, pencils or paper circles to the camera. Ask participants to answer in chat before revealing the number sentence.
    07 / Your practical homework

    20-minute rehearsal at home.

    Act as teacher, learner and observer. The best way to understand the framework is to experience the learning process yourself.

    🧺

    1. Collect simple props

    20 bottle caps, beans or blocks, 4 paper plates, a pencil and paper. No expensive kit required.

    🎭

    2. Play three roles

    Be the teacher first, then the child, then the observer. Speak the dialogue aloud and move the objects.

    📝

    3. Record evidence

    Write one thing the learner understood, one strategy used and one next step you would teach.

    Official references & accuracy note

    The lesson, role-play scripts and rubric are original practice examples, not official government templates. They are informed by the following sources:

    • National Education Policy 2020 · holistic and experiential learning; 5+3+3+4 structure.
    • National Curriculum Framework for Foundational Stage 2022 · play, activity-based pedagogy and early mathematics.
    • PARAKH, NCERT · competency-based assessment and Holistic Progress Cards.
    • NCF-FS: Teaching Mathematics · problem-solving, reasoning, representation, communication and estimation.

    NEP is the national policy; NCF is a curriculum framework; HPC means Holistic Progress Card. PARAKH is the assessment centre established within NCERT. Adapt the examples and assessment expectations to the learner's age, stage, curriculum and school context.
